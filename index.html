Paddlesport & raft building permits

How leaders gain and use them in Scouts

Why permits matter

  • Kayaking, canoeing, SUP and traditional raft building on the water are adventurous activities for young people.
  • You need the appropriate permit to lead or supervise on the water.
  • Permits keep young people safe and activities within POR and activity guidance.

Paddlesport permits

  • Personal — for under-18s to take part for themselves.
  • Leadership — lead one group (e.g. up to 8 craft or 12 people); stay on the water with them.
  • Supervisory — oversee more than one group with clear communication and rescue cover.
  • Kayak, canoe and stand-up paddleboard permits are separate where required.

Raft building (traditional)

  • Pioneering rafts from poles, barrels and rope — then paddling on the water.
  • A traditional rafting permit is required except on Class C water (safe inland water under 100 m wide).
  • Same permit types: personal, leadership and supervisory — matched to water class (B1, B2, B3, A).
  • Assessment covers raft construction, loading, launching, paddling, safety and rescue.

Before you apply

  • Adults need a suitable role, safeguarding checks and (18+) a DBS where required.
  • Speak to your Commissioner or Lead Volunteer about your plans.
  • Build and log experience on the craft and water you want to lead.
  • NGB qualifications may reduce practical assessment — you still need an assessor recommendation.

Path to a permit

  • Find an Activity Assessor for your activity and environment.
  • Complete assessment against the official checklist — skills, safety and Scout rules.
  • Assessor recommends a level in My Membership.
  • Your Lead Volunteer checks and grants the permit; renew about every five years.
